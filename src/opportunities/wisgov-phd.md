WISGOV (Water Integration for Systems Governance) is a four-year project funded by the EPA Research
Programme 2030. Ireland's water agencies plan for floods, water quality, water supply and farming
largely separately. WISGOV will build a national integrated water-cycle model for Ireland with
[WSIMOD](/software/wsimod/), and use it with the EPA, Uisce Éireann, OPW, LAWPRO and DAFM to test
adaptation and governance options.

This PhD delivers the national-scale modelling. A central challenge is karst: limestone groundwater
that strongly shapes Irish catchments but isn't represented in any existing integrated water systems
model. You will:

- couple Irish karst groundwater models into WSIMOD, and develop a simple karst module that works
  nationally
- calibrate and validate the national model, using uncertainty and sensitivity analysis to check it
  is right for the right reasons
- help turn stakeholders' objectives into scenarios they can explore, and take part in workshops

You'll work with a Research Fellow at Trinity and a PhD student at Dublin City University working on
water governance.

## Who we're looking for

- A first or upper second-class honours degree, or a master's, in engineering, hydrology, earth or
  environmental science, physical geography, maths, physics, computer science or a related subject
- Programming experience, or keenness to learn, and confidence analysing data
- An understanding of hydrological, hydrogeological or environmental processes

Experience of hydrological or groundwater modelling, karst, GIS, Git or uncertainty analysis is a
plus, but not essential. If English isn't your first language, you'll need to meet Trinity's
[English language requirements](https://www.tcd.ie/courses/postgraduate/how-to-apply/entry-requirements/).
